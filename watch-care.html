Care guide

The watch care guide

Simple habits for winding, cleaning, storing and servicing your watch, plus the most common mistakes and how to avoid them.

Everyday habits

Wearing and winding

  1. Take it off before winding. Winding on the wrist puts sideways pressure on the crown stem. Remove the watch first.
  2. Wind until resistance. For a manual watch, stop when you feel firm resistance. Never force it further.
  3. Screw-down crowns. If your crown screws down, always push it in and screw it closed after use to keep water out.
  4. Set the date safely. Avoid quick-setting the date between about 9 pm and 3 am, unless your manual says it is safe.
  5. Avoid knocks. Shock protection helps, but hard impacts from golf, tennis or tools can still damage the balance.

Cleaning the case

Wipe with a soft microfibre cloth. For water-resistant watches with sealed crowns, a soft toothbrush and lukewarm water can clean a metal bracelet. Dry thoroughly.

Leather straps

Keep them away from water, perfume and prolonged sweat. Let a strap air out overnight and rotate between straps in hot weather.

After the sea

Rinse in fresh water to remove salt and sand, especially around the bezel and bracelet, then dry with a soft cloth.

Storage

When it is off your wrist

  • Store watches in a dry place at room temperature, away from direct sunlight.
  • Keep them away from speakers, magnetic clasps, tablets and phone cases with magnets.
  • Use a soft-lined box or individual pouches so watches do not scratch each other.
  • An automatic watch does not need to run constantly. A winder is a convenience, not a requirement.
  • If storing a manual watch for a long time, wind it fully every few weeks to keep lubricants moving.
  • For quartz watches, replace a dead battery promptly; old batteries can leak and damage the movement.

Servicing

What happens at a full service

A full service usually involves dismantling the movement, cleaning every part, replacing worn components, re-lubricating, reassembling and regulating it for accuracy. The case is cleaned, gaskets are replaced and water resistance is tested.

Choose an experienced watchmaker, ask for a written estimate and ask whether they will keep or return original parts. Keep the service paperwork with the watch; it is useful history for the future.

Advice here is general. Always follow the specific guidance for your watch.
